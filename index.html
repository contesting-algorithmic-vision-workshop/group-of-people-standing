A Group of People Standing

Workshop: Contesting Algorithmic Vision
November/December 2025

A Group of People Standing explores how Instagram’s AI-generated alt-text classifies images, often flattening cultural, political, and emotional meaning into neutral machine-readable descriptions. The workshop connects research and communication design to question how algorithmic image interpretation shapes visibility, climate narratives, and our understanding of reality.

Workshop Results

These projects explore how climate discourse is shaped by both human communication and algorithmic systems. From hashtags and posting strategies to large-scale image analysis, they reveal how platforms classify, filter, and frame climate content. Together, the works expose the invisible mechanisms behind digital visibility and offer a critical perspective on how climate narratives are constructed today.

RONI LEVIT, OMER ELIRAZ, RON PORAT, DARIA BEN DROR
Climate Tags
Identifying the key associations surrounding climate-related discourse on social platforms
This project examines the secondary tags used in social media posts that include the climate hashtag. The aim is to identify key associations surrounding climate-related discourse on social platforms. To communicate these findings, a series of infographic posters was designed, each highlighting a distinct trend revealed by the data.
MEIR SADAN, Hadar Ginbank, YUVAL MATSIL, INBAL LUZ, ROY AMIT
The Perfect Post
A Guidebook for Instagram Climate Activists
This guide started from a simple question: what makes a <> climate post actually work? Step by step, it broke the post into its parts - * image, timing, *e hashtags and captionand looked at how each one behaves in real #climate and #nature feeds.
NICOLE MUSHAILOV, YUVAL MATSIL, MICHAL HARGEV, NOYA ANTMAN RON, ROMI BAUCH, OMRI BERGMAN
Hue of Crisis
A Group of People Standing Workshop Project
Hue of Crisis examines how the climate crisis is represented in images from online news media. Based on an extensive dataset of climate-related visuals, the project analyzes patterns across color, time, location, and image content. An interactive interface enables filtering and exploration of the data, allowing both an overview of broader trends and close inspection of individual images. Through this approach, the project reveals visual patterns and underlying structures in how climate narratives are constructed and communicated.
LAMAR KHAMASHTA
Alt-text
Experimenting with Instagram’s Auto Alt-Text and Climate Engagement
This project investigates how Instagram’s auto alt-text interprets posts related to climate and nature, focusing on the impact of text overlays on engagement. Through data analysis and visual experimentation, it explores how algorithmic image descriptions shape visibility, communication, and awareness around climate content.
